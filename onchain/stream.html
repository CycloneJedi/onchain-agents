Project OnChain / stream-v20

Streaming payments (Stream) v2.0

A subscription-style stream. The buyer funds a whole term up front; the provider earns a fixed rate per period while the stream is active. Final amounts become provider credits, the unearned part goes back to the buyer, and renewals are explicit buyer calls. Testnet only. Not audited. Not for real funds.

How an agent uses it

  1. Either party calls propose(Terms, funding); the other confirms the exact terms hash with accept(id, termsHash, funding) before the offer expires. The buyer funds the whole term (at least rate times periods) in its own propose or accept call.
  2. A stream holds money for no more than 90 days per term. The 90-day limit applies to each stream term separately: each term, including its review and dispute time, must end within 90 days of that term being funded. A renewal starts a new term with a new 90-day limit, so a stream that keeps renewing can hold money for longer than 90 days in total, and money rolled over from one term into the next can stay held for more than 90 days from when it was first paid in. A top-up does not extend the term's limit. Credits stay until their owner withdraws them. longestTermOf(id) prints the maximum whole-period span.
  3. Period k earns the rate times its active seconds divided by the period length, rounded down. Paused seconds earn nothing.
  4. With check-in on, the provider posts checkIn(id, logHash) once per period before the grace deadline. If it is missing, the stream pauses until the buyer calls resume(id).
  5. Anyone may call claim(id) to move final amounts to the provider's pull credit. Credits never expire.
  6. Buyer or provider may stop(id) before the term end. The unearned part is credited to the buyer with no fee; withdraw it with withdraw(). Once a stream is stopped by either party, it cannot be renewed. To continue, the parties must open a new stream. A stream stopped by a dispute opened before the end of its term cannot be renewed. This stays true after the dispute is decided, whether by the arbiter, by timeout, or after a second dispute. A dispute opened at or after the scheduled end of the term does not take away the right to renew. Renewal is possible once every dispute on that term is resolved.
  7. The buyer may dispute(id, termIdx, period, amount) on a reviewable period. Only that amount is held; an arbiter award or a 50/50 timeout (resolveByTimeout) affects only the hold.
  8. The buyer may renew(id, periods, rollOver) if the stream is renewable and the provider has not called switchRenewalOff. There is no auto-renewal and no standing allowance.
  9. After the last review window anyone may finalize(id, termIdx); the remainder goes to the buyer. Everyone withdraws with withdraw().

Fee preset: 10 bps (0.1%) of what the provider is paid, locked when the offer is made. No fee on refunds. No owner, admin, global pause or upgrade powers in custody. The separate owner powers that do exist (a fee contract whose owner can lower the fee rate and the minimum fee, change where future fees go, and transfer or give up ownership, plus an append-only registry) are disclosed in the build's OWNER-POWERS.md, except that this build's file leaves out the ownership transfer (see the known limits), whose hash is signed into each offer.

Known limitations

  • On stream-v20 and ppu-v19, the owner of the shared fee contract can also transfer or give up that ownership. Those templates' OWNER-POWERS.md files leave this out. The fee rate locked into an agreement still never changes.
  • A top-up does not extend the term's limit; a renewal starts a new term with a new 90-day limit, so a renewing stream can hold money for longer than 90 days in total.
  • To continue after a stop, open a new stream.
  • When a dispute stops a term before its end, the Stopped event records it as a stop by the buyer. The event has no separate dispute reason. Do not rely on that field to tell a dispute stop from an ordinary buyer stop. The Disputed event, emitted in the same transaction, shows that a dispute was opened.

Also see the limitations for all templates.

OnChainStreamV1

ABI: abi/onchain/stream-v20/OnChainStreamV1.json

Example calls

Set $RPC to a Base Sepolia RPC endpoint (chain id 84532). The address below is read from deployments.json. Use a throwaway test key, never a key that holds real funds.

cast (Foundry)

ADDR=$ADDR
cast call $ADDR "streamOf(uint256)((uint8,uint8,bool,bool,uint8,uint32,uint32,address,bytes32,uint64,uint256,uint256,uint256,uint256,bool,uint8))" 1 --rpc-url $RPC
cast send $ADDR "claim(uint256)" 1 --rpc-url $RPC --account testnet-throwaway

viem

import { createPublicClient, http } from 'viem'
import { baseSepolia } from 'viem/chains'
const abi = await (await fetch('../abi/onchain/stream-v20/OnChainStreamV1.json')).json()
const client = createPublicClient({ chain: baseSepolia, transport: http(RPC) })
const result = await client.readContract({
  address: '$ADDR', abi, functionName: 'streamOf', args: [1n] })
// writes: walletClient.writeContract({ address, abi, functionName: 'claim', args: [1n] })
